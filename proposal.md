# AI at work: a transition pilot for two occupations

**Policy client and context:** This brief is for the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR) and its AI Employment and Workplaces Forum, following DEWR's 2026 *AI and employment in Australia* report and the National AI Plan.

## Executive Summary

AI is changing the work of contact-centre and records staff, but Australia does not yet have evidence of widespread job losses. DEWR found slower employment growth in more AI-exposed occupations; it cannot say AI caused the gap. This brief proposes an 18-month pilot in these two occupations. Participating workplaces would involve staff before changing their tasks and offer human review of consequential AI-assisted decisions. DEWR and Jobs and Skills Australia would track jobs, pay and work quality. Up to 2,000 workers could receive paid training linked to actual vacancies, if the evidence shows a need. The estimated Commonwealth ceiling is A$17 million, including A$8 million held back until that need is established. An independent evaluation should decide whether the pilot warrants wider use, taking employer costs into account.

## Problem Definition and Context

AI can draft replies, search records and suggest how work is assigned. Some tasks may need fewer staff. Faster service and new tasks could create demand elsewhere ([Acemoglu and Restrepo 2019](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.3)).

[DEWR's July 2026 report](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/17666/ai-and-employment-australia/43205/ai-and-employment-australia/pdf) found employment grew 5.6% in the most AI-exposed fifth of occupations between November 2022 and February 2026, compared with 9.5% in the least-exposed fifth. It found no broad disruption. The statistical link weakens under some alternative exposure measures, and older occupational trends may explain part of the gap. There is reason to prepare for adjustment, but not to predict mass job losses.

DEWR's [2026–27 corporate plan](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/17793/department-employment-and-workplace-relations-corporate-plan-2026-2027/43700/department-employment-and-workplace-relations-corporate-plan-2026-2027/pdf) says its AI Employment and Workplaces Forum will bring together government, employers and unions. The [National AI Plan](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/national-ai-plan/spread-benefits) calls for training and worker input. Many awards and agreements already require consultation on major technology changes. That does not necessarily give a worker a way to inspect or challenge an individual decision made with an algorithm ([Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/tools-and-resources/best-practice-guides/consultation-and-cooperation-in-the-workplace)).

## Analysis of Impacts

Contact-centre staff may find answers faster, leaving more time for difficult cases. At one US firm, an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 15%, especially for newer staff. Australian workplaces may get different results ([Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044)). Records workers may spend less time filing and more time checking classifications and fixing errors. [Jobs and Skills Australia (JSA)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study/our-gen-ai-transition-labour-market-dynamism) finds no clear evidence yet that generative AI has caused a general decline in entry-level jobs.

JSA's ABS-based profiles count [28,500 call or contact-centre workers](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations-anzsco/5411-call-or-contact-centre-workers) and [16,300 filing and registry clerks](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/data/occupation-and-industry-profiles/occupations-anzsco/5613-filing-and-registry-clerks) in February 2026. Women account for 64% and 71% respectively. Median full-time weekly earnings in May 2025 were A$1,414 and A$1,602, below A$1,852 across occupations.

Who gets the productivity gains is an open question. Pay might rise, or customers and firms might keep most of the benefit. Automated performance targets could also make work more pressured. The [ABS business survey](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/2024-25) found AI use in 12% of businesses in 2024–25. Among innovation-active firms, 19% of small firms used AI, compared with 37% of large firms. If adoption stays uneven, larger providers may gain work. The pilot should include different firm sizes and regions, and check whether casual staff can get training.

## Policy Options

The costs below are rough 18-month Commonwealth estimates. Employers would also bear some staff-time costs.

| Option: mechanism and target | Efficiency | Equity | Feasibility | Fiscal cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Paid transition training:** offer up to 2,000 affected workers a vacancy-linked course and one paid study week; pay a worker allowance or reimburse an employer that releases staff without cutting pay. | Medium: useful only if it improves job matches. | High: paid time helps workers who cannot train unpaid. | Medium: requires suitable courses and vacancies. | **A$8m:** 2,000 × (A$2,000 course + A$1,500 paid time), plus A$1m delivery. |
| **B. Consultation and review pilot:** support 100 employers and worker representatives to map AI-affected tasks, disclose how consequential work scores are used, and provide a documented human review route. | Medium: may avoid errors and poor job redesign. | High: gives affected staff a way to challenge decisions. | Medium: needs access to system information. | **A$7m:** 100 × A$50,000 for paid staff time, system review and procedure design, plus A$2m evaluation. |
| **C. Extend DEWR/JSA monitoring:** add observed adoption, wages, hours, vacancies and worker experience to existing occupation analysis. | High: improves targeting at modest cost. | Medium: information alone does not compensate workers. | High: builds on current work. | **A$2m:** five staff × A$180,000 × 1.5 years, plus A$650,000 for data. |

A$2,000 per course is a planning figure. A [DEWR briefing](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/16962/official-department-employment-and-workplace-relations-pack-used-free-tafe-bill-2024-inquiry-friday/39943/official-dewr-pack-used-free-tafe-bill-2024-inquiry-friday-7-february-2025/pdf) estimated Commonwealth contributions of about A$1,380 for existing Fee-Free TAFE places and A$3,460 for additional places, though course length and state funding differ. A$1,500 for paid time is roughly one week's gross pay in the pilot occupations. DEWR would need to test the other rates through tenders.

## Recommended Package and Implementation Plan

DEWR should fund B and C first (A$9 million) and hold A$8 million for training. It should release that money only when JSA and the Forum find changed tasks, a training gap and suitable vacancies. The maximum new Commonwealth cost is A$17 million. This is a capped pilot budget bid, with no automatic national rollout. [Existing digital-skills training](https://www.dewr.gov.au/skills-education-and-employment) is available; the new funding should cover only extra support tied to jobs.

In months 1–3, DEWR and JSA should choose sites and agree with employers and unions on data access. Months 4–6 establish a baseline for tasks, pay and AI use. Workplaces test consultation and review in months 7–15. If demand for training exceeds the available places, eligible workers could be offered places by lottery. The workplace protocol could be introduced in stages. These approaches would create comparison groups while keeping existing rights in place. At month 18, an independent evaluator should report on job retention, redeployment, real hourly pay, hours, worker autonomy, substantiated decision errors and cost per sustained improvement. DEWR should expand the pilot only if it outperforms existing services once public and employer costs are counted.

## Risks and Mitigations

An occupation can be exposed to AI even where employers do not use it. Collect workplace-use data and test more than one exposure measure. Employers might claim grants for training they would have paid for anyway; require evidence of a gap and a contribution from larger firms. If a vendor will not explain a work score, workers cannot properly challenge it. Participating employers must provide usable explanations and record reasons for decisions. Publish selection rules and results by firm size, region and employment type. Stop any component that costs more without improving workers' outcomes.
