# AI use reflection — draft

I used Codex while planning this proposal. It helped me find primary sources, compare five Australian departments and sketch three problems and policy options for each. I used it again to organise the DEWR material under the assessment headings. These were research and planning uses, so I still need to check the sources and decide which argument I can defend.

One Codex draft went too far: it wrote a near-finished policy brief when I wanted working dot points. I set that version aside and asked for an outline. That mattered because polished prose can make uncertain evidence and rough cost assumptions look settled. DEWR's finding that highly AI-exposed occupations grew more slowly is a useful lead, but it does not prove AI caused the difference.

I also asked Codex to apply the Humanizer skill to the new drafts. It helped remove stiff, repetitive wording; it did not verify a citation or improve an estimate. Before I commit the final report, I will question the research claims, check the cost arithmetic and rewrite any recommendations I do not agree with. The main risk I noticed is how quickly a plausible AI-generated policy package can outrun the evidence behind it.
