# AI use reflection

Codex was useful early in this assessment. I used it to sort lecture ideas, find government and academic sources, and sketch three problems and three options for each of five portfolios. After choosing DEWR, I used it to structure the brief, check links and word counts, and tidy the Markdown and Git files. It helped edit the final prose. I treated the linked DEWR, ABS and JSA material as evidence, rather than Codex's summaries. The US productivity study stayed an example, not a forecast for Australia.

I pushed back on the first proposal. It came back as polished prose when I still needed dot points to think through the choices. I kept that version as a backup and asked for an outline. Later, when the research was clearer, I asked for a full draft. That sequence reminded me how easily fluent writing can make weak evidence sound settled. DEWR reports slower growth in more AI-exposed occupations, but cannot say AI caused the gap.

The hardest part was checking the numbers behind a plausible story. The A$17 million package is a transparent estimate, not an official costing. I chose the recommendation and remain responsible for the final argument and commits.
